      VV, VH, and the VV/VH ratio

      A SAR instrument sends a microwave pulse and listens for the echo (backscatter). Polarization names the electric-field direction: VV is vertical transmit and vertical receive; VH is vertical transmit and horizontal receive (cross-pol).

      Smooth open water acts like a mirror. The pulse reflects away from the satellite (specular reflection), so VV backscatter drops and water looks dark. Cross-pol (VH) is already weak over smooth water, so the VV/VH ratio (VV minus VH in dB) helps separate wind-roughened water, wet soil, and vegetation.

      The false-color product in the viewer is exactly that: red = VV, green = VH, blue = VV/VH, stretched with a shared 2–98 percentile per orbit so the time-lapse does not flicker.

      Dielectric constant and roughness

      Radar is most sensitive to two things: surface roughness relative to the wavelength, and the dielectric constant. Liquid water has a very high dielectric constant, so wet soil and inundation change the return even when the geometry stays the same. That is why a dark VV patch after 29 October is physically meaningful, not just a contrast stretch.

      Why NISAR L-band can see flood under a canopy

      C-band (Sentinel-1) interacts strongly with leaves and small branches. In orchards, marshes, and the Albufera wetland, C-band often never reaches the water under the vegetation, so a flood can be missed. L-band (NISAR) is about four times longer. It penetrates deeper into the canopy and can return a double-bounce signature from trunk and water — a brighter, not darker, response in inundated forest.

      That contrast — C-band dark open water versus L-band canopy-penetrating inundation — is the story this challenge is built for. Saturday's Earthdata .h5 hook is how we put real NISAR polarizations on the same map.

      What this viewer is (and is not)

      Every Valencia frame is a real Sentinel-1 RTC GeoTIFF, downsampled for the web, with VV, VH, and VV/VH rendered from the same pixels. Before/after pairs are always the same orbit and the same grid. The change map is a VV dB difference, not a neural-network mask.

      The U-Net numbers in the Metrics tab were measured on Sen1Floods11, not on these Valencia tiles. Valencia is the storytelling AOI; Sen1Floods11 is the labelled test set. We do not pretend otherwise.
